Why we built this

The problem

Control is one question. Risk is the other.

Toxic cyanobacteria blooms can close beaches, threaten drinking water, and harm pets and wildlife. Several treatments can control a bloom, and choosing between them is a real decision.

Jordan Lake, North Carolina, June 2022

Dogs became ill after swimming at Jordan Lake, and at least one died. Chatham County issued a public warning about toxic algae in the water, and told people to keep themselves and their pets out of it.

ABC11: dog death linked to water toxins at Jordan Lake

Treatments are usually compared on how well they control the bloom. This tool adds a comparison of predicted risk to green algae, zooplankton, and fish, to help identify the lowest-risk option.

Each of the three groups is scored because it plays a distinct role in the lake:

Green algae

Primary producers at the base of the food web, and the standard non-target algae in toxicity testing.

Water fleas

Zooplankton that graze algae and feed fish, which makes them part of the lake’s natural check on algae.

Fish

The visible top of the system, and the reason most people care about the lake in the first place.

Most comparisons ask which treatment controls the bloom. This tool also asks which one does it with the lowest predicted risk to everything else.

We started with the selectivity ranking: which treatment has the widest safety margin for the three groups a lake manager wants to keep, the green alga, the water flea and the fish. Then we realized a ranking from lab tests can't tell a manager what happens in their own pond, with their own weather, after they spray. Where does the product go? Does it reach the bloom? How much toxin comes out, and does the oxygen crash?

So we added the simulator. Our goal is one free place where a manager can compare treatments, test a dose and an application method on their own pond, and see the trade-offs before treating. Today the ranking compares treatments and the simulator follows one treatment plan at a time. Comparing doses and methods side by side is where the project is heading.

Not a treatment recommendation. Every number here is a model prediction from published lab data. Always follow the product label and your state's rules.

Part 1

The ranking math

How it works

Score the trade-off, keep the axes separate, then predict the pairs.

The tool combines two established methods (selectivity indices from toxicology and Bliss independence from combination pharmacology) into one framework for bloom control. Three steps, no black boxes.

1

Score

Selectivity Index = non-target EC50 divided by target EC50

SI = EC50 (bystander) ÷ EC50 (bloom)

EC50 is the dose that causes a 50% effect. It runs backwards from intuition: a low EC50 means harm arrives at a small dose, so a low number is a sensitive organism, not a safe one.

The ideal treatment kills the bloom at a low dose and only touches the bystanders at a much higher dose. That gap is the selectivity window, and SI measures it as a single number.

SI above 1: good

The bloom dies first. There is a dose that controls the bloom before the bystander is meaningfully harmed.

SI below 1: flagged

In the published tests, the bystander responded at a lower dose than the bloom did. The tool flags that axis.

2

Separate

Three non-target axes, tracked independently and never averaged

Green alga

Raphidocelis subcapitata

The primary producer. If it goes, the food web loses its base.

Water flea

Daphnia magna

The grazer. It eats algae and feeds fish, so it sits in the middle of everything.

Fish

Danio rerio

The vertebrate check. Standard proxy for vertebrate safety, including ours.

A treatment can score well for one group and poorly for another. Copper is the clearest case: it looks excellent against the green alga and much weaker against the water flea. Average those two numbers together and you get one comfortable-looking score that hides exactly the trade-off a lake manager needs to see.

So the axes are reported separately, and the headline number is min-SI, the worst axis of the three.

You are only as selective as your weakest link.

3

Combine

Predict what treatment pairs will do, before anyone runs the experiment

effect(A+B) = A + B − (A × B)

This is Bliss independence: the effect of A, plus the effect of B, minus the overlap you would otherwise count twice. It assumes the two agents act independently, which is a stated assumption rather than a claim.

Why it matters here: since the headline score is the weakest axis, the way to improve a treatment is to lift that axis. Pair something strong on the green alga axis with something strong on the water flea axis and the pair can outrank either one alone. The tool generates every single treatment and every pair, scores them on the same scale, and ranks them together.

The selectivity index

How many times more product it takes to hit a bystander than to hit the bloom.

\[ \mathrm{SI} = \frac{\mathrm{EC50}_{\text{bystander}}}{\mathrm{EC50}_{\text{bloom}}} \]
SymbolMeaningUnits
\(\mathrm{EC50}\)The concentration that causes a 50% effect in a lab test of a set lengthmg/L
\(\mathrm{SI}\)Above 1: the bloom is hit first. Below 1: the bystander responded at a lower dose, and the axis is flaggednone

Values from the CyanoSelect dataset of published lab tests (Data). Label: the EC50s are SOURCED; each SI is DERIVED.

Rank by the weakest margin, never the average

A treatment is only as selective as its worst axis, so the headline score is the smallest of its three.

\[ \text{min-SI} = \min\left(\mathrm{SI}_{\text{green alga}},\ \mathrm{SI}_{\text{water flea}},\ \mathrm{SI}_{\text{fish}}\right) \]

Averaging hides the trade-off. Copper is the clearest case: its green-alga score is 29.7 but its water-flea score is 3.4. A geometric mean puts copper near the top; the weakest axis shows the water flea is exposed. The ranking tool lets you switch between the two and watch the order change.

Predicting pairs: Hill curves and Bliss independence

Each product's effect rises along an S-shaped curve; two products acting independently add their effects, minus the overlap you would otherwise count twice.

\[ E(c) = \frac{(c/\mathrm{EC50})^{n}}{1 + (c/\mathrm{EC50})^{n}}, \qquad n = 1 \]
\[ E_{A+B} = E_A + E_B - E_A\,E_B \]

Equipotent mixing. Each product in a pair is dosed at the same multiple \(s\) of its own bloom EC50. On the bloom, each then has an effect \(E(s; 1)\); on a bystander axis, product \(A\) has \(E(s; \mathrm{SI}_A)\). The pair's score on that axis is the dose that gives a 50% bystander effect divided by the dose that gives a 50% effect on the bloom:

\[ \mathrm{SI}_{A+B} = \frac{s_{50}\big[\,E(s;\mathrm{SI}_A) + E(s;\mathrm{SI}_B) - E(s;\mathrm{SI}_A)\,E(s;\mathrm{SI}_B)\,\big]}{s_{50}\big[\,2E(s;1) - E(s;1)^2\,\big]}, \qquad E(s;R) = \frac{s/R}{1 + s/R} \]
SymbolMeaningValue
\(n\)Hill slope of every curve1 (an assumption)
\(s\)Dose as a multiple of each product's bloom EC50none
\(s_{50}[\cdot]\)The \(s\) at which that effect reaches 50%, found by bisectionnone

Bliss independence assumes the two products act independently. Products that share a mechanism (copper and hydrogen peroxide both act partly through oxidative stress) may not. Pairs are predictions and have not been tested in a lab. Label: DERIVED from the SOURCED EC50s, under stated assumptions.

Part 2

The simulator math

The simulator takes one pond, the weather and one treatment, and predicts the next 14 days hour by hour. It has three views of the same pond:

  • Well-mixed: one stirred tub, or just the warm top layer if the pond is layered.
  • Layers: a stack of thin slices, 12 to 40 of them, about 6 inches each.
  • Map and side slice: about 450 squares seen from above, with sideways currents, and a vertical cut along the wind. They track 1,000 colonies one by one.

The physics comes from published equations. Each card says whether its numbers are SOURCED (read in the cited paper), DERIVED (worked out from sourced numbers or standard physics) or an ESTIMATE (a reasonable value with a range, where no measurement was found).

Pond bowl shape

Most ponds are bowl-shaped: the area of water deeper than a given depth shrinks as you go down.

\[ A(z) = A_0\left(1 - \frac{z}{z_{\max}}\right)^{p}, \qquad p = \frac{1}{r} - 1, \qquad \text{share of water above depth } h = 1 - \left(1 - \frac{h}{z_{\max}}\right)^{p+1} \]
SymbolMeaningUnitsValue
\(A_0\)Surface aream²your pond
\(z_{\max}\)Deepest watermyour pond
\(r\)Average depth ÷ deepestnone0.40 (0.30–0.50), so \(p = 1.5\)

Source: Seekell et al. 2021 (lakes average about 0.41). ESTIMATE Enter your average depth to replace it.

Light with depth

Sunlight fades exponentially with depth, faster in green or muddy water. Cells crowded near the surface shade the water below them.

\[ I(z) = I_0\,e^{-K_d z}, \qquad K_d = \frac{1.7}{z_{\text{Secchi}}} + 0.1\,\mathrm{NTU}, \qquad z_{\text{Secchi}} = \exp\!\left(\frac{60 - (9.81\ln \mathrm{Chl} + 30.6)}{14.41}\right) \]

Heat budget

Every 5 minutes the layers model adds up the heat coming in and going out at the surface: sunlight, sky radiation, the water's own radiation, contact with the air and evaporation.

\[ \text{Sky radiation (Brutsaert):}\quad \varepsilon_a = \min\!\left\{1,\ 1.24\left(\frac{e_a}{T_a}\right)^{1/7}\!\left(1 + 0.22\,c^2\right)\right\}, \qquad L_{\text{in}} = \varepsilon_a \sigma T_a^4, \quad L_{\text{out}} = 0.985\,\sigma T_s^4 \]

\[ C_H(U) = 1.3\times10^{-3}\left(1 + 1.5\,e^{-0.8U}\right), \qquad C_E(U) = 1.1\times10^{-3}\left(1 + e^{-U}\right) \]

Mixed-layer depth from wind and cooling

At night the cooling surface sinks and mixes. By day, the wind's stirring deepens the warm top layer whenever it has enough energy to lift the denser water just below; energy that falls short is saved for later steps.

\[ E_{\text{wind}} = c_k\,\rho_0\,u_*^3\,\Delta t\,A_0, \qquad E_{\text{needed}} = g\,\Delta\rho\,\frac{V_m V_k}{V_m + V_k}\,\big(z_k - \bar z_m\big), \qquad u_* = \sqrt{\frac{\rho_a C_D(U)\,U^2}{\rho_0}} \]
SymbolMeaningUnitsValue

\(u_*\)The water's friction velocity from the wind stressm/sabout 0.003 in the example
\(C_D(U)\)Drag coefficient, \(1.7\times10^{-3}(1 + e^{-1.1U})\)nonerises in light wind
\(\Delta\rho\)How much denser the next layer down iskg/m³
\(V_m,\ \bar z_m\)Stirred layer's volume and centre of massm³, m
\(V_k,\ z_k\)Next layer's volume and depthm³, m

The energy balance follows the General Lake Model (Hipsey et al. 2019). The stirring efficiency and the minimum depth are ESTIMATEs. Trees cut the wind for about 50 tree heights downwind of the shore (Markfort et al. 2010).

Eddy diffusivity: how strongly water mixes up and down

Turbulence in the wind-stirred layer is weak at the surface and at the bottom of the layer and strongest in the middle. Below the thermocline, mixing drops to near the molecular level.

\[ K(z) = \max\big\{K_{\min},\ \kappa\,u_*\,z\,(1 - z/D)\big\} \quad (z < D), \qquad K = 8.17\times10^{-4}\,A^{0.56}\,(N^2)^{-0.43}\ \text{cm}^2/\text{s} \quad (\text{below}) \]

Wind counterflow

The wind drags the top water downwind. It has nowhere to go, so it flows back underneath: as much water returns as goes forward.

\[ u(\zeta) = u_s\,(3\zeta^2 - 2\zeta), \qquad u_s = \frac{1.5\,u_*}{\kappa} \approx 3.7\,u_* \]
SymbolMeaningUnitsValue
\(\zeta\)Height in the stirred layer: 0 at its bottom, 1 at the surfacenone
\(u_s\)Surface speed, about 0.5% of the windm/s1.1 cm/s in the example

The flow is zero two-thirds of the way up and returns below that at up to a third of the surface speed. The very top centimetre moves faster still, dragged directly by the wind: 0.53 times the air's friction velocity (0.4–0.7; Wu 1975, SOURCED), fading over about 1 cm (ESTIMATE).

The profile comes from the eddy viscosity \(\kappa u_* D/6\) with water that sticks to the bottom of the layer. DERIVED

Thermocline tilt and the Wedderburn number

In a layered pond the wind piles warm water against the downwind shore, so the boundary under it tilts. If the tilt is steep enough, cool water comes up at the upwind shore (upwelling).

\[ \text{slope} = \frac{u_*^2}{g' h}, \qquad W = \frac{g' h^2}{u_*^2 L}, \qquad g' = g\,\frac{\Delta\rho}{\rho_0}, \qquad t_{\text{set-up}} = \frac{L}{2c}, \quad c = \sqrt{\frac{g' h_1 h_2}{h_1 + h_2}} \]
SymbolMeaningUnits
\(g'\)Gravity scaled by the density step across the thermoclinem/s²
\(h\) (or \(h_1\)), \(h_2\)Warm-layer thickness; cool water below itm
\(L\)Length of the pond along the windm
\(W\)Wedderburn number: below 1, upwelling at the upwind shorenone

The tilt eases toward its steady value over a quarter of the internal sloshing period, \(t_{\text{set-up}}\). The sloshing after the wind changes is not modelled. DERIVED

The topographic swirl (streamfunction)

Shallow water is easier for the wind to push than deep water, so the edges move downwind and the deep middle flows back upwind in two slow loops.

\[ \nabla\cdot\left(\frac{r}{D^2}\,\nabla\psi\right) = -\,\hat{\mathbf z}\cdot\nabla\times\left(\frac{1.5\,\boldsymbol\tau}{\rho_0\,D}\right), \qquad \mathbf U = \left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y},\ -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\right), \qquad \psi = 0 \text{ at the shore} \]
SymbolMeaningUnitsValue
\(\psi\)Streamfunction: the flow between two points is the difference in \(\psi\)m³/s
\(\mathbf U\)Depth-integrated flowm²/s
\(r\)Bottom friction, from the same eddy viscositym/s\(\kappa u_*/2\)
\(\boldsymbol\tau\)Wind stress; the bed's drag adds half again (1.5×)N/m²
\(D\)Depth of the stirred water in each squarem

A flat surface ("rigid lid") and linear friction leave this single equation, solved over the whole map at once. Using a streamfunction guarantees exactly as much water leaves each square as enters it; the tests check that to about 1 part in 10¹⁵. DERIVED Sideways mixing, 0.0002 m²/s per metre of pond size (from Lake Constance drifters, Peeters 2015), is an ESTIMATE.

Colony rise (Stokes' law)

Microcystis colonies have gas pockets and float, at a speed set by their size and how much lighter than water they are.

\[ w = \frac{g\,(\rho_w - \rho_c)\,d^2}{18\,\mu(T)} \]
SymbolMeaningUnitsValue
\(\rho_c\)Colony densitykg/m³985 (Zhao 2017, SOURCED)
\(d\)Colony diameterµm200 (100–400), ESTIMATE
\(\mu(T)\)Water viscosity at the water temperaturePa·sVogel's formula

A 200-micron colony rises about 1 m (3.5 ft) per hour. Planktothrix is treated as neutral; Anabaena gets a slow estimated rise. The colonies don't sink by day and rise at night yet.

The colony random walk

On the map, each tracked colony takes a random step up or down every minute or two, sized by the mixing strength at its depth, so that colonies that simply follow the water stay evenly spread (Visser 1997).

For the mixing profile above, \(K = \kappa u_* z(D - z)/D\), a colony's depth behaves exactly like a dot wandering over a ball, with depth read off its height: \(z = \tfrac{D}{2}(1 - \cos\theta)\). Each step turns the ball by a random angle \(\rho\) in a random direction \(\phi\):

\[ \cos\theta' = \cos\theta\cos\rho + \sin\theta\sin\rho\cos\phi, \qquad \rho = \sqrt{-\frac{4\kappa u_*\Delta t}{D}\ln U_1}, \qquad \phi = 2\pi U_2 \]
SymbolMeaningUnits
\(\theta\)The colony's position on the ballradians
\(U_1,\ U_2\)Random numbers between 0 and 1none
\(\Delta t\)Step lengths (30–180)

Each step has the same average size and spread as Visser's (1997) random walk. Turning a ball keeps an even spread exactly even, whatever the step length.

Changed Nov 8, 2026. Until then the model used Visser's step directly. With these step lengths it left the top and bottom few percent of the stirred water 20–33% too full of colonies. A calm-water floor, \(K_{\min}\), is added as a separate even step. DERIVED

The damage model (GUTS) and the share knocked down

In a pond the concentration spikes and fades, so the model turns a changing exposure into damage inside the cells. A cell is knocked down when its damage passes its own toughness.

\[ \frac{dD}{dt} = k_d\,\big(C(t) - D\big), \qquad F_{\text{knocked down}} = \frac{1}{1 + \big(m / D_{\max}\big)^{\beta}} \]
SymbolMeaningUnitsValue
\(D\)Damage, in concentration unitsmg/L
\(k_d\)Damage rate1/h0.45 (0.1–2), ESTIMATE
\(D_{\max}\)Highest damage reached so farmg/L
\(m\)Median toughness, set so the lab test gives exactly 50% at its EC50 and test lengthmg/Lcalibrated
\(\beta\)How spread out the toughness isnone2 (1–4), ESTIMATE

GUTS (General Unified Threshold model of Survival) is the standard framework in ecotoxicology. This is its tolerance rule. The other rule, sudden death, knocks cells down at random at a rate \(b_w\max(0, D - z)\); the page shows it as a separate scenario. The peroxide test dosed once and let it fade, so the calibration replays that fading dose, at an estimated 0.46 per hour. "Knocked down" means photosynthesis or growth stopped, which is what the lab measured, not confirmed death.

Toxin release and breakdown

Cells hold most of their toxin inside. Knocked-down cells leak it into the water. Bacteria break dissolved toxin down after a lag, and sunlight slowly breaks it down in the stirred top water.

Oxygen

The living bloom makes oxygen by day and breathes day and night; everything else in the pond is sized so the untreated pond is in balance. A knock-down stops the bloom's oxygen while the pond keeps breathing, which is the main cause of fish kills after a treatment.

The page reports the lowest hour, usually just before dawn, against fish-stress (4 mg/L) and fish-kill (2 mg/L) levels. It shows oxygen for the wind-stirred water only; bottom water in a layered pond can go much lower, and that isn't modelled yet.

With the idealized day, every day has the same weather and the pond is balanced to it, so an overcast run can show more oxygen than a sunny one. The real crash risk is a cloudy spell after sunny weather, which only real weather input can show.

The 200 Latin hypercube runs, and what the band means

Many numbers are uncertain, so the layers and well-mixed models run 200 times, each run drawing every uncertain number from its range.

Each range is cut into 200 slices of equal chance. Every slice is used exactly once, in a shuffled order, so each range is covered evenly (a Latin hypercube). Ranges spanning more than tenfold are sampled on a log scale. The charts show the median run and a band from the 5th to the 95th percentile of the 200 runs.

The band covers uncertain numbers only. It doesn't cover the model's own structure: the other kill rule (shown separately), the three views' different simplifications, or the estimates the runs hold fixed. Read it as the smallest the real uncertainty could be. The map and side slice run once, with the central value of every number.

Changed Nov 8, 2026. Until then there were 40 runs. With 40, the median knock-down moved by about ±6 points with the random seed; with 200 it moves by about ±3.

What it doesn't do yet

The simulator

  • No check against a real pond. The tests show the math does what it says. Matching a real pond needs before-and-after measurements in a treated pond.
  • Real weather comes from a weather model for the area, not a station at the pond. Forecasts lose skill after about a week, and a sheltered pond can be calmer than the area's wind.
  • No inflows or outflows.
  • No waves, and so no wave drift on top of the surface current.
  • No sloshing of the thermocline after the wind changes (internal seiches); the tilt is the steady one.
  • No plume right at an injection hose: the dense layer starts already spread over each treated square.
  • No colonies sinking a little by day and rising at night.
  • Copper's harm to animals follows hardness only, not the dissolved organic carbon or pH that also matter.
  • The idealized day's sunshine is an estimate, and the stirred layer has a 2.5-ft minimum by day. On a run of sunny days the top of a 6-ft pond warms about 11 °F by afternoon, at the high end of what small ponds show.
  • Pond size changes the wind only through tree shelter; an open pond mixes the same at any size.
  • No oxygen in the cool bottom water of a layered pond.
  • In a strong wind, colonies that simply drift with the water can still gather up to about 10% too thickly along the shores of the map. In the side slice, colonies at the very top move at the top level's average speed rather than the faster top centimetre.

The ranking

Pairs are predictions, the input data is sparse (15 of 52 target values exist), test lengths don't always match, Bliss independence can fail, and the ranking scores selectivity, not how well a product controls the bloom. The full list is on the ranking page.

How we checked it

284automated tests
35scenarios in a separate Python version
23checks of the page in a real browser
  • 284 automated tests (74 for the well-mixed model, 96 for the layers model, 114 for the map and side slice). They check that:
    • a fully mixed pond gives the same answer as the well-mixed model;
    • stronger wind never makes the stirred layer shallower;
    • the lab calibrations give exactly 50% at each EC50;
    • colonies that simply follow the water stay evenly spread.
  • An independent Python version of the well-mixed model, written separately from the website's code, matches it to 1 part in 10¹⁰ across 35 scenarios.
  • Mass balance. Product, cells and toxin add up to within 1 part in 10¹⁰ for all 13 combinations of product and application method, in all three views: nothing appears or disappears.
  • Convergence. We halved and quartered the time step, used 12 to 40 layers, doubled the number of tracked colonies and ran up to 1,000 uncertainty runs. That is why the layers model now takes 5-minute steps and the page runs 200 times.
  • Cited values against their sources. Every value labelled SOURCED was checked against the paper it cites. Where a paper couldn't be opened, the simulator's notes say so, and values without a checkable citation are labelled ESTIMATE, citation pending.

What the tests can't show is that the predictions match a real pond. That needs real pond data: product concentrations at a few spots and depths after a treatment, plus before-and-after bloom counts and oxygen readings. If you can help with that, tell us.
