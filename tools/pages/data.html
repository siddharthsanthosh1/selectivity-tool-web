What's next: data

Fill in the missing data

Only 15 of 52 target-kill cells are currently filled. Some of those gaps are genuine absences in the literature, and some are values locked behind paywalls or buried in regulatory documents. Every cell filled makes the ranking more stable. Priorities are additional target-kill values for the three under-covered cyanobacteria genera, and cross-validating the values that currently rest on a single source.

One of four next steps. The others: lab validation, outreach and scope.

Three findings

What shows up once you actually score the trade-off.

None of these are visible when treatments are compared on bloom control alone.

Finding 01

Copper's effective dose rises with bloom density.

Published work (Tsai 2016; Kinley et al. 2017) shows the concentration needed to control Microcystis rises as cell density increases. That narrows copper's margin against Daphnia, which is already its weakest axis in the ranking. Copper's score is the one most sensitive to the conditions of a real bloom.

SI 3.4Daphnia axis, at test cell density

Finding 02

The ranking depends on which data you pair.

Our first version paired a short-test average across five cyanobacteria with a longer growth test on a single green alga, and ranked diquat last. Using matched values from one study (Peterson et al. 1997), diquat ranks second. Mismatched test lengths and endpoints can move a treatment from last to near first.

6th to 2nddiquat rank, mismatched vs. matched data

Finding 03

The data to answer this question barely exists.

Compiling published toxicity data across 13 treatments and 4 cyanobacteria genera, only 15 of 52 target-kill cells could be filled. The non-target side of the ledger is comparatively rich, because harm to fish, water fleas, and green algae is what regulatory testing is built to measure. How well a chemical kills the bloom itself is measured far less consistently.

Labels

What each label means.

The simulator lists every number it uses, with its source and one of three labels. The same words appear on the simulator's How it works pages.

SOURCED

Read in the cited paper or dataset. Where we could not open a source to check it, the simulator's notes say so.

DERIVED

Worked out from sourced numbers or standard physics, like the speed of the wind-driven counterflow.

ESTIMATE

A physically reasonable value with a range, where no measurement was found, or where the citation is still pending. The uncertainty runs vary it across its range.
