Worked example: diquat against Microcystis

Diquat's bloom EC50 and its three bystander values, all from published tests.

OrganismEC50 (mg/L)SourceSI
Microcystis (bloom)0.101Peterson et al. 1997 (0.098 and 0.105, same carbon-uptake test)—
Green alga0.492Peterson et al. 1997, same study and test4.9
Water flea (Daphnia)1.86U.S. Environmental Protection Agency ECOTOX database18.4
Fish50.2U.S. Environmental Protection Agency ECOTOX database497
\[ \mathrm{SI}_{\text{green alga}} = \frac{0.492}{0.101} = 4.9, \quad \mathrm{SI}_{\text{water flea}} = \frac{1.86}{0.101} = 18.4, \quad \mathrm{SI}_{\text{fish}} = \frac{50.2}{0.101} = 497 \]

Diquat's weakest margin is the green alga, so its min-SI is 4.9 (4.87 unrounded). Scored on the two lab-testable axes, it ranks first among single treatments. Paired with copper sulfate, whose weak axis is the water flea instead, the predicted pair scores 6.24, above either product alone.

Framework complete. Laboratory validation planned.

Model predictions, not a treatment recommendation. CyanoSelect ranks treatments from published lab data and simulates ponds; none of its predictions has been tested in a real pond yet. Registered products have undergone risk assessment by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, and the product label governs how they may be used. Follow the label and your state’s rules, and consult a licensed aquatic applicator or your state agency before treating any water body.

Built by Arjun Pendharkar and Siddharth Santhosh, Green Level High School, under the supervision of Dr. Bharathi Pandi. This site describes research tools whose predictions have not yet been validated in a laboratory or a real pond, and it is not guidance for treating a waterbody.
