The selectivity index

How many times more product it takes to hit a bystander than to hit the bloom.

\[ \mathrm{SI} = \frac{\mathrm{EC50}_{\text{bystander}}}{\mathrm{EC50}_{\text{bloom}}} \]
What the symbols mean
SymbolMeaningUnits
\(\mathrm{EC50}\)The concentration that causes a 50% effect in a lab test of a set lengthmg/L
\(\mathrm{SI}\)Above 1: the bloom is hit first. Below 1: the bystander responded at a lower dose, and the axis is flaggednone

Values from the CyanoSelect dataset of published lab tests (Data). Label: the EC50s are SOURCED; each SI is DERIVED.

Rank by the weakest margin, never the average

A treatment is only as selective as its worst axis, so the headline score is the smallest of its three.

\[ \text{min-SI} = \min\left(\mathrm{SI}_{\text{green alga}},\ \mathrm{SI}_{\text{water flea}},\ \mathrm{SI}_{\text{fish}}\right) \]

Averaging hides the trade-off. Copper is the clearest case: its green-alga score is 29.7 but its water-flea score is 3.4. A geometric mean puts copper near the top; the weakest axis shows the water flea is exposed. The ranking tool lets you switch between the two and watch the order change.

Predicting pairs: Hill curves and Bliss independence

Each product's effect rises along an S-shaped curve; two products acting independently add their effects, minus the overlap you would otherwise count twice.

\[ E(c) = \frac{(c/\mathrm{EC50})^{n}}{1 + (c/\mathrm{EC50})^{n}}, \qquad n = 1 \]
\[ E_{A+B} = E_A + E_B - E_A\,E_B \]

Equipotent mixing. Each product in a pair is dosed at the same multiple \(s\) of its own bloom EC50. On the bloom, each then has an effect \(E(s; 1)\); on a bystander axis, product \(A\) has \(E(s; \mathrm{SI}_A)\). The pair's score on that axis is the dose that gives a 50% bystander effect divided by the dose that gives a 50% effect on the bloom:

\[ \mathrm{SI}_{A+B} = \frac{s_{50}\big[\,E(s;\mathrm{SI}_A) + E(s;\mathrm{SI}_B) - E(s;\mathrm{SI}_A)\,E(s;\mathrm{SI}_B)\,\big]}{s_{50}\big[\,2E(s;1) - E(s;1)^2\,\big]}, \qquad E(s;R) = \frac{s/R}{1 + s/R} \]
What the symbols mean
SymbolMeaningValue
\(n\)Hill slope of every curve1 (an assumption)
\(s\)Dose as a multiple of each product's bloom EC50none
\(s_{50}[\cdot]\)The \(s\) at which that effect reaches 50%, found by bisectionnone

Bliss independence assumes the two products act independently. Products that share a mechanism (copper and hydrogen peroxide both act partly through oxidative stress) may not. Pairs are predictions and have not been tested in a lab. Label: DERIVED from the SOURCED EC50s, under stated assumptions.

Framework complete. Laboratory validation planned.

Model predictions, not a treatment recommendation. CyanoSelect ranks treatments from published lab data and simulates ponds; none of its predictions has been tested in a real pond yet. Registered products have undergone risk assessment by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, and the product label governs how they may be used. Follow the label and your state’s rules, and consult a licensed aquatic applicator or your state agency before treating any water body.

Built by Arjun Pendharkar and Siddharth Santhosh, Green Level High School, under the supervision of Dr. Bharathi Pandi. This site describes research tools whose predictions have not yet been validated in a laboratory or a real pond, and it is not guidance for treating a waterbody.
