Pond bowl shape

Most ponds are bowl-shaped: the area of water deeper than a given depth shrinks as you go down.

\[ A(z) = A_0\left(1 - \frac{z}{z_{\max}}\right)^{p}, \qquad p = \frac{1}{r} - 1, \qquad \text{share of water above depth } h = 1 - \left(1 - \frac{h}{z_{\max}}\right)^{p+1} \]

Source: Seekell et al. 2021 (lakes average about 0.41). ESTIMATE Enter your average depth to replace it.

Light with depth

Sunlight fades exponentially with depth, faster in green or muddy water. Cells crowded near the surface shade the water below them.

\[ I(z) = I_0\,e^{-K_d z}, \qquad K_d = \frac{1.7}{z_{\text{Secchi}}} + 0.1\,\mathrm{NTU}, \qquad z_{\text{Secchi}} = \exp\!\left(\frac{60 - (9.81\ln \mathrm{Chl} + 30.6)}{14.41}\right) \]
What the symbols mean
SymbolMeaningUnitsValue

1.7\(K_d\) × Secchi depthnone1.7
0.1Extra loss per unit of mineral turbidity (mud only)1/m per NTU0.1 (0.05–0.2)

The Secchi formula is Carlson's 1977 trophic state index (SOURCED). The 1.7 factor is a standard value, citation pending, and the turbidity term has no pond calibration: ESTIMATE Enter a Secchi depth to replace both.

Heat budget

Every 5 minutes the layers model adds up the heat coming in and going out at the surface: sunlight, sky radiation, the water's own radiation, contact with the air and evaporation.

\[ \text{Clear-sky sunlight (Haurwitz):}\quad G = 1098\,\sin h\;e^{-0.057/\sin h} \]
\[ \text{Sky radiation (Brutsaert):}\quad \varepsilon_a = \min\!\left\{1,\ 1.24\left(\frac{e_a}{T_a}\right)^{1/7}\!\left(1 + 0.22\,c^2\right)\right\}, \qquad L_{\text{in}} = \varepsilon_a \sigma T_a^4, \quad L_{\text{out}} = 0.985\,\sigma T_s^4 \]
\[ H = \rho_a c_p \max\{C_H(U)\,U,\ v_f\}\,(T_s - T_a), \qquad E = \rho_a L_v \max\{C_E(U)\,U,\ v_f\}\,(q_s - q_a) \]
\[ C_H(U) = 1.3\times10^{-3}\left(1 + 1.5\,e^{-0.8U}\right), \qquad C_E(U) = 1.1\times10^{-3}\left(1 + e^{-U}\right) \]
What the symbols mean
SymbolMeaningUnits
\(h\)Sun's height above the horizondegrees
\(e_a,\ T_a,\ c\)Air vapour pressure, air temperature, cloud coverhPa, K, 0–1
\(T_s\)Water surface temperatureK
\(U\)Wind speed 10 m upm/s
\(v_f\)Calm-air (free convection) exchange speed, as in the General Lake Modelm/s
\(\rho_a,\ c_p,\ L_v\)Air density 1.2, its heat capacity 1005, heat of evaporation 2.45×10⁶kg/m³, J/(kg·K), J/kg
\(q_s,\ q_a\)Specific humidity at the water surface and in the airkg/kg

Sources:

  • Haurwitz (1098 and 0.057): SOURCED.
  • Brutsaert (1.24 and 1/7): SOURCED. The sky reading is capped at a perfect radiator.
  • The cloud factor \(1 + 0.22c^2\): ESTIMATE citation pending.
  • Transfer coefficients: Guseva et al. 2023, measured over 31 lakes and reservoirs. SOURCED, but we could not open the paper's Table 1 to check the exact values.
  • Calm-air factor: ESTIMATE
  • The idealized day's sunshine, 75% of clear sky (60–90%): ESTIMATE

Mixed-layer depth from wind and cooling

At night the cooling surface sinks and mixes. By day, the wind's stirring deepens the warm top layer whenever it has enough energy to lift the denser water just below; energy that falls short is saved for later steps.

\[ E_{\text{wind}} = c_k\,\rho_0\,u_*^3\,\Delta t\,A_0, \qquad E_{\text{needed}} = g\,\Delta\rho\,\frac{V_m V_k}{V_m + V_k}\,\big(z_k - \bar z_m\big), \qquad u_* = \sqrt{\frac{\rho_a C_D(U)\,U^2}{\rho_0}} \]
What the symbols mean
SymbolMeaningUnitsValue
\(c_k\)Wind-stirring efficiencynone0.23 (0.15–0.35)
\(u_*\)The water's friction velocity from the wind stressm/sabout 0.003 in the example
\(C_D(U)\)Drag coefficient, \(1.7\times10^{-3}(1 + e^{-1.1U})\)nonerises in light wind
\(\Delta\rho\)How much denser the next layer down iskg/m³
\(V_m,\ \bar z_m\)Stirred layer's volume and centre of massm³, m
\(V_k,\ z_k\)Next layer's volume and depthm³, m
\(h_{\min}\)Smallest stirred depth by daym0.75 (0.5–1), about 2.5 ft

The energy balance follows the General Lake Model (Hipsey et al. 2019). The stirring efficiency and the minimum depth are ESTIMATEs. Trees cut the wind for about 50 tree heights downwind of the shore (Markfort et al. 2010).

Eddy diffusivity: how strongly water mixes up and down

Turbulence in the wind-stirred layer is weak at the surface and at the bottom of the layer and strongest in the middle. Below the thermocline, mixing drops to near the molecular level.

\[ K(z) = \max\big\{K_{\min},\ \kappa\,u_*\,z\,(1 - z/D)\big\} \quad (z < D), \qquad K = 8.17\times10^{-4}\,A^{0.56}\,(N^2)^{-0.43}\ \text{cm}^2/\text{s} \quad (\text{below}) \]
What the symbols mean
SymbolMeaningUnitsValue

\(K_{\min}\)Background mixing in calm waterm²/s1×10⁻⁵ (3×10⁻⁶–1×10⁻⁴)

Below the thermocline, mixing follows the Hondzo & Stefan 1992 regression, kept between the molecular value (1.4×10⁻⁷ m²/s, Lang & Scavia 1987) and \(K_{\min}\). The calm-water floor is an ESTIMATE.

Model predictions, not a treatment recommendation. CyanoSelect ranks treatments from published lab data and simulates ponds; none of its predictions has been tested in a real pond yet. Registered products have undergone risk assessment by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, and the product label governs how they may be used. Follow the label and your state’s rules, and consult a licensed aquatic applicator or your state agency before treating any water body.
