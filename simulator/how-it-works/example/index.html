Pond Simulator · How it works

Worked example: one acre, one heavy bloom, one spray of peroxide.

The simulator's built-in example pond, and what the model predicts for it. These are model predictions, not measurements: nothing here has been tested in a real pond yet.

The pond:

  • 1 acre, 8 ft at the deepest point, round, 82 °F water.
  • Open banks, with a 4.5 mph wind from the southwest on a sunny mid-July day.
  • A heavy Microcystis bloom (100 µg/L chlorophyll).
  • Liquid hydrogen peroxide sprayed on the whole pond at 3 mg/L at 8 AM.
What the model predictsValueHow firm it is
Top-water peak right after sprayingabout 26 mg/LThe map's highest top-water square in the first hour. The layers model's top 6 inches peak at about 20 mg/L.
Bloom knocked down by day 2 (median of 200 runs)about 61–67%The page shows 67%. The median moves between about 61% and 67% with the random seed; 1,000 runs give 61%.
Knock-down, 5th–95th percentileabout 4% to 98%Knock-down is the most uncertain prediction, because no one has measured how fast these chemicals damage cells over time.
Knock-down on the map (one central run)63%
Total toxin vs the swimming advisory level (8 µg/L)above it all 14 daysStarts at about 54 µg/L in the top water, nearly 7 times the level, and never falls below about 16. The cells still alive hold their toxin, and released toxin breaks down slowly.
Lowest-hour oxygen (median of runs)3.7 mg/LBelow the 4 mg/L fish-stress level. The 5th–95th percentile is 1.6–5.2 mg/L; in the worst 5% of runs it falls to about 1 mg/L, low enough to kill fish.
Lowest-hour oxygen, map and layers model4.5 and 4.1 mg/LOne central run each.

These are the numbers after the November 2026 checks, which found:

  • the earlier median of 64% (8–98% across 40 runs) depended on the random seed;
  • the 10-minute time step cost the layers model about 2.7 points.

Model predictions, not a treatment recommendation. CyanoSelect ranks treatments from published lab data and simulates ponds; none of its predictions has been tested in a real pond yet. Registered products have undergone risk assessment by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, and the product label governs how they may be used. Follow the label and your state’s rules, and consult a licensed aquatic applicator or your state agency before treating any water body.

Built by Arjun Pendharkar and Siddharth Santhosh, Green Level High School, under the supervision of Dr. Bharathi Pandi. This site describes research tools whose predictions have not yet been validated in a laboratory or a real pond, and it is not guidance for treating a waterbody.
