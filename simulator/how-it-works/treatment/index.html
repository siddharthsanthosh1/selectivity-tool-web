Hydrogen peroxide decay

Bacteria and algae break peroxide down, faster in warm water and in a thick bloom. Part of the dose is also used up instantly.

\[ \frac{dC}{dt} = -k\,C, \qquad k = 1.078^{\,T - 20}\,\big(k_0 + b\,\mathrm{Chl}\big) \]

Each step uses the exact exponential. In the example the top water loses half its peroxide in about 1¼ hours. Your own measured half-life replaces the rate.

Flumioxazin breakdown with pH

Flumioxazin breaks down in water, much faster at high pH, and a bloom's photosynthesis raises the pH every afternoon.

\[ k = k_0 + k_{\text{OH}}\,10^{\,\mathrm{pH} - 14} \]

Diquat binding to clay

In muddy water, clay takes up diquat, most of it within the first hour, slowing as the clay fills up.

\[ \frac{db}{dt} = k\,C\left(1 - \frac{b}{b_{\max}}\right), \qquad b_{\max} = 0.6 \times \frac{1.5\,\mathrm{NTU}}{1000} \times \mathrm{CEC} \times 92.1 \]

With \(C + b\) fixed over a step, this is solved exactly, in every layer and square the diquat reaches. After that, diquat leaves the water at 0.5 per day (0.35–0.7; Wilson & Wu, SOURCED range).

Copper and water hardness

Copper is far more toxic to fish and water fleas in soft water, so their effect levels are scaled to your water's hardness.

\[ \mathrm{EC50}(H) = \mathrm{EC50}_{\text{lab}}\left(\frac{H}{100}\right)^{0.9422}, \qquad 10 \le H \le 400\ \text{mg/L} \]
What the symbols mean
SymbolMeaningUnitsValue
\(H\)Your water's hardness (taken as the alkalinity if blank)mg/L as CaCO₃

Green algae and the bloom aren't adjusted, because the rule is for animals. Copper itself leaves the water at a measured fish-pond rate, \(k = (0.761 + 0.0018\,(\text{alkalinity} - 78))\cdot 1.09^{\,T - 26.8}\) per day (Marple et al. 2001, DERIVED).

Framework complete. Laboratory validation planned.

Model predictions, not a treatment recommendation. CyanoSelect ranks treatments from published lab data and simulates ponds; none of its predictions has been tested in a real pond yet. Registered products have undergone risk assessment by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, and the product label governs how they may be used. Follow the label and your state’s rules, and consult a licensed aquatic applicator or your state agency before treating any water body.

Built by Arjun Pendharkar and Siddharth Santhosh, Green Level High School, under the supervision of Dr. Bharathi Pandi. This site describes research tools whose predictions have not yet been validated in a laboratory or a real pond, and it is not guidance for treating a waterbody.
