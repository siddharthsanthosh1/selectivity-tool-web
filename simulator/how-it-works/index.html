Pond Simulator · How it works

A lab ranking can't tell you what happens in your pond.

So we built a simulator that follows one treatment through one pond, with real weather, for 14 days. Here is why and what it predicts. The worked example and every equation are in the chapters that follow.

Why we built it

The ranking came first. It wasn't enough.

We started with the selectivity ranking: which treatment has the widest safety margin for the three groups a lake manager wants to keep, the green alga, the water flea and the fish. Then we realized a ranking from lab tests can't tell a manager what happens in their own pond, with their own weather, after they spray. Where does the product go? Does it reach the bloom? How much toxin comes out, and does the oxygen crash?

So we added the simulator. Our goal is one free place where a manager can compare treatments, test a dose and an application method on their own pond, and see the trade-offs before treating. Today the ranking compares treatments and the simulator follows one treatment plan at a time. Comparing doses and methods side by side is where the project is heading.

Not a treatment recommendation. Every number here is a model prediction from published lab data. Always follow the product label and your state's rules.

What it predicts

What it predicts

Where the product goes, how much of the bloom is knocked down, toxin released into the water, oxygen through day and night, and how close green algae, water fleas and fish come to their lab effect levels.

What you need

Your pond's area and deepest point, water temperature, pH, alkalinity and cloudiness, the bloom's chlorophyll, and the product, dose, method and time. For weather, give a place and date, or describe one typical day. Every other field is optional.

The simulator takes one pond, the weather and one treatment, and predicts the next 14 days hour by hour. It has three views of the same pond:

  • Well-mixed: one stirred tub, or just the warm top layer if the pond is layered.
  • Layers: a stack of thin slices, 12 to 40 of them, about 6 inches each.
  • Map and side slice: about 450 squares seen from above, with sideways currents, and a vertical cut along the wind. They track 1,000 colonies one by one.

The physics comes from published equations. Each card says whether its numbers are SOURCED (read in the cited paper), DERIVED (worked out from sourced numbers or standard physics) or an ESTIMATE (a reasonable value with a range, where no measurement was found).

Model predictions, not a treatment recommendation. CyanoSelect ranks treatments from published lab data and simulates ponds; none of its predictions has been tested in a real pond yet. Registered products have undergone risk assessment by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, and the product label governs how they may be used. Follow the label and your state’s rules, and consult a licensed aquatic applicator or your state agency before treating any water body.

Built by Arjun Pendharkar and Siddharth Santhosh, Green Level High School, under the supervision of Dr. Bharathi Pandi. This site describes research tools whose predictions have not yet been validated in a laboratory or a real pond, and it is not guidance for treating a waterbody.
